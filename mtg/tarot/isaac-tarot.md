0 - Fool - 10/10. The major arcana are often viewed through the idea that they comprise a story of growing stronger/smarter/better/coming of age/etc. Very vague (on purpose) but the fool here is the starting point. This character, this archetype, along the journey is literally the **starting point.** I don’t think you can do better at making form meet function.
1 - Magician - 8/10. This card represents seeing a glimpse of one’s potential. This magician is a person like you, but cooler and more capable. They have greater control over their path in life. They don’t miss shots like a stupid baby.
2 - High Priestess - 1/10. This card represents something much more subtle than this effect implies. It’s about intuition, this priestess is so in tune with herself and her surroundings, there’s much to learn from her, etc. A big stomp she is not.
3 - Empress - 3/10. Why the fuck does this turn you into a demon?? I know the whole theme with the game is that it loves heavy handed religious iconography, but like, the card that just represents “a motherly figure” makes you a demon. I know Isaac doesn’t have a great relationship with his mom but jeez kid. **WHY DOES THIS ONE NOT SUMMON THE MOM STOMP???** Why does the high priestess do that instead????
TLDR: The in-game effect is neither the intended tarot meaning nor as related to Isaac’s mom as it could be, big failure all around.
4 - Emperor - 0/10. If we’re going to have the empress obviously be related to Isaac’s view of his mom, why does this not somehow reference his dad? From my memory, I guess the only mentions of a father are in like, the Polaroid and stuff, and I guess those are usually down deeper into the dungeon, and this takes you to the hatch that leads down. It also represents authority, and declaring that you’re ready to fight a boss is, I guess, authoritative. There are a lot of mental gymnastics you can do to say that this is flavorful, but this is weak sauce any way you slice it.
5 - Hierophant - 2/10. This is a man of faith. He represents community and structure and staying within one’s reach. The soul hearts are like, kinda-sorta-faith-related, if you squint.
6 - Lovers - 3/10. This card is deeply related to twos, and obviously there’s the heart motif inherent in love. However, these two lovers are supposed to represent a kind of balance or choice. A common example is that one lover is weak while the other is strong, or that they cover each other’s weaknesses. Balance is easy, just make the card spawn one red heart and one blue heart.
7 - Chariot - 6/10. This card represents primarily willpower, and is associated with winning. So you have a few moments of invincibility, I guess? I think this card should make you unable to die for like 2 minutes or some effect like that.
8 - Justice - 1/10. Fun fact, this card is only 8 in the popular Rider-Waite tarot deck, for dumb reasons you can google. The fact that this is 8 means that isaac is using an unusually old/especially traditional deck. Some of the details on their artwork indicates that isaac uses this deck: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarot_of_Marseilles .
This card is about karma, both good and bad, and about balancing intuition in one's actions with logic. This card should give you what you're **missing**, maybe? It just gives you one of everything. How the hell is that justice? There are OTHER tarot cards that would make a good fit for the message of "you have what you need with you already". There isn't even an attempt to make this feel connected.
9 - Hermit - 0/10. This card is usually interpreted to mean that you should look inside yourself and do some introspection. You should carefully contemplate things. What you should NOT do is go on a spending spree???? Go to Shop and Buy Items??? This card should slow down time for a little bit instead.
10 - Wheel of Fortune - 5/10. This card does kinda evoke luck, even if it tries to be about fate instead. So it spawns a slot machine, sure. But then you can just not use the slot machine. You actually have quite a lot of control over your fate with this card. Just like, give it the effect of one of the dice rooms/items or something.
11 - Strength - 8/10. This card means you are strong, patient, fearless, resilient. The effect is pretty blatant. Interestingly, this is the card that Justice got swapped with in most decks vs Rider-Waite decks, and also the card I would suggest giving Justice's effect to.
12 - Hanged Man - 0/10. Why am I fucking bothering with this shit, the dev just makes isaac's head fly off because decapitation is another similar form of execution to hanging. No, this card has nothing to do with that. It's about taking a step back to better prepare, or reevaluate yourself.
13 - Death - 0/10. Look, everyone always looks at this card and sees the big scary skeleton and thinks it's literally about dying. It's not, it's about transformation. Something should be lost and something should be gained. I'd be cool with this card if it created a new enemy in addition to damaging the old ones, but it doesn't do that. This card could also just have the effect of one of the dice rooms/items, which are **already programmed into the game**, but it doesn't.
14 - Temperance - 7/10. The temperance card is supposed to symbolize calm sanctity in all that one already has, you're supposed to forgo excess in some way. The effect does allow you to give up any excess hearts you have.
15 - Devil - 0/10. You got it backward, this card shouldn't make you more powerful. The idea is that the devil is here and harming you somehow, usually by making you feel powerless or oppressed or dependent on some vice. My headcanon for the in-universe reason that this makes you more powerful is that, every time you use the card, isaac quits smoking. Again.
16 - Tower - 5/10. Not much to say here, the tower is pretty much just destructive and dangerous. I will say that this effect is not very inspiring, but it *does* have those qualities. Maybe have it just immediately kill most enemies at the cost of one of your items?
17 - Star - 6/10. This is why I suggested that effect with the tower. The whole idea with the star is that the light is at the end of the tunnel, and you will be given what you are owed by the universe in time. It's juxtaposed with the tower a lot. So having the tower take away an item and then the star provide you with one would be really nice, and it's a missed opportunity that I think is deeply obvious if you sit down and think about these cards.
I'd bump both of these cards up a couple points if they mirrored each other like that. (But let's not get our hopes up, the dev named this item "The Stars" even though no decks do that, and the card only gives you one item as represented by the one star that should be there.)
18 - Moon - 8/10. This card often means that there's some kind of hidden truth for you to discover, or it could be a caution that you are taking actions without knowing their full consequences. The effect here is riffing on the first meaning, and it's alright. No complaints. I would have given it a higher rating if it purely opened up the secret room, but didn't teleport you there, because you have yet to discover it.
19 - Sun - 9/10. When you draw the sun card, that's awesome. It means that everything's going great for you, broadly speaking. Yeah, this is the right effect. I would have loved a little detail that it would also heal any enemies in the room as well, but it doesn't do that.
20 - Judgement - 4/10. This card usually indicates that you'll be faced with choices and have to evaluate yourself. This card does face you with a choice, but that choice is just, do I want to spend a shiny gold coin. Weak.
21 - World - 2/10. Why is this just the sun but worse. Could this guy seriously not think of another effect that represents, like, "i'm done, i've seen all there is to see, there is nothing left to achieve"? You did a bad job. This effect is boring and barely relevant.

SUMMARY
Edmund McMillen scrolled through a wikipedia page for a tarot deck one day. He said to himself that the most popular deck was lame, and used the second most popular deck instead. He blithely read some but not all of the meanings provided on the wikipedia articles for each card, and probably stopped there. He misread some of the names and numbers, and accidentally shuffled the order of some of the cards, and only fixed these cosmetic issues when Rebirth came out. At that point, he had a great opportunity to change what some of the cards did, and decided not to take it.
Some of these cards seem to indicate that their effects are based on Isaac's understanding of the world around him, which would have been an interesting place to go. Unfortunately, it's basically only the empress and high priestess cards that do this, along with a few very christian interpretations of other cards like judgement and temperance. The hanged man in particular is dumb as hell, so I believe that it's the intended interpretation that these effects come from Isaac's flawed, incomplete understanding of these cards' interpretation.
