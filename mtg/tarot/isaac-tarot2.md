Reversed tarot cards have meanings that are basically completely arbitrary and only vaguely related to their upright meanings. They are a level of such bullshit that a lot of people that read tarot cards as a profession can't even abide. Like, tarot is already a lot of wishy-washy whatever you want to hear shit, but nobody agrees on these completely. My standards for these are very, very low, because pretty much anyone can have a valid opinion on how to read these cards reversed as long as their interpretation A) Somehow riffs on the upright meaning, and B) flips it somehow along ANY axis whatsoever.

0 - Fool? - 8/10. Yeah, pretty good. This card emphasizes the other side of the fool, that this character at the start of their journey is completely unprepared and weak and without help.
1 - Magician? - 7/10. Obviously this is literally just the opposite effect to what the card usually does. This card reversed could represent illusions and trickery.
2 - High Priestess? - 1/10. One singular point for mirroring its upright effect, but like what the HELL does this have to do with tarot. Normal high priestess is, somehow, related to listening to one's intuition. Is it... unintuitive...? that mom shows up and stomps you?
3 - Empress? - 1/10. Okay, instead of being the whore of babylon, the opposite of that is magdalene, I guess. I don't think this has anything interesting to say beyond that. In fact, in retrospect, it kinda makes my whole "the tarot effects are based on isaac's perception" take less supported. The opposite of the archetypal mother figure, in his fucked up baby head, is mary magdalene. There's so many references to her being a prostitute in the game (which on some level is its own dramatic irony, because we in the audience know that's apocryphal.) So: The spectrum in isaac's head for what it's even possible for a mother to be goes from:
Demon ----------------------- Shamed Prostitute (we hate prostitutes in this catholic shithole house)
And then it ends there. Why do you not become angelic or something? It's not like christianity is completely bereft of respected mother figures, either. Jesus has a pretty popular mom in catholic churches, from what I understand. There was a LOT of stuff they could have done, but didn't, and it doesn't even support my fucked up stupid excuse that I made up to make edmund mcmillen seem smart!
4 - Emperor? - 7/10. A lot of people denote a reversed emperor as being an ABUSE of authority or power. Or like, some kind of overreach, or that you lack control of your situation. I kinda like the idea that you're voluntarily going up against a harder opponent than you "should" be, and it does neatly mirror the upright effect.
5 - Hierophant? - 3/10. The hierophant is literally a pope. It's like, the most catholic-coded card in the deck. I understand the soul heart thing, but I really wish that this card or its upright version had something more. Once again, my whole "isaac's perception" idea has a pretty big hole. What is this, an evil pope who gives you evil faith? No, that would be black hearts, surely. Bone hearts were just a new mechanic that they wanted to provide a source for. Black hearts come from all of the sacrilege effects in the game. To talk about the actual reversed tarot interpretation here, where the hierophant represents order and tradition, it's very easy to paint the reversed hierophant as the opposite. There's really nothing you can think of to make that work, Edmund? Why not just have it spawn some random hearts of any kind?
6 - Lovers? - 7/10. Where the regular Lovers represents balance in a lot of ways, inverted you can have it mean instability or disharmony of some kind, so like, your heart is permanently enshittified. I can look past the random extra effect that it gives you an item, because obviously you need some kind of upside for this card.
7 - Chariot? - 10/10. Where the regular Chariot says that you have control over your destiny and the power to bring yourself to victory, this card yoinks away your controls and makes your targets tantalizingly out of reach for those 10 seconds. Very clever.
8 - Justice? - 1/10. I can see that this card mirrors the effect of regular justice. What the hell is the flavor text even talking about? Is it a reference to the chest, like the level "The Chest"? To be honest, the regular actual justice tarot card meaning doesn't really lend itself to reversing. It's a reminder that "your actions have consequences", and as you can kinda see with this flavor text, the only really satisfying way to reverse that is "your actions WILL have consequences (ominous)". Seriously, reversed tarot cards require so much mental gymnastics to try and satisfy yourself with.
9 - Hermit? - 1/10. Where the normal Hermit represents solitude, an inner voice as a wellspring of wisdom, shit like that. Reverse it, and like, maybe solitude is bad, or your conscience is misleading you, or all kinds of things. This card just gives you Big Money. Why does it give you money.
10 - Wheel of Fortune? - 7/10. Just like justice, this one doesn't lend itself to reversal. How do you invert a card that means "you are not in control of your destiny"? You can't say that you ARE in control, that's the chariot. So most people just take it to mean that your luck ain't gonna be too great soon, which is... partially satisfying. Anyway, props to edmund for using the dice rooms. That is an extremely sensible thing to use.
11 - Strength? - 5/10. Alright, sure, it just applies the opposite effect but to enemies this time. There's not much to say here. Inverse strength means you don't have strength. Not especially inspiring, but understandable.
12 - Hanged Man? - 0/10. This one still has a noose, sure, awesome. That's where the relationship ends. The only "mirroring" that happens here is that you transform into a different, broadly unrelated thing. Regular hanged man is like "you are taking a step back and steadying yourself or meditating somehow, so that you will see rewards in the future." The regular card in-game makes you actually much better at approaching others and you get no coins for doing so. So the inverted card in-game has an effect better suited to the upright version of the in-game card.
13 - Death? - 0/10. AGAIN this is a better effect for the original card and vice versa. This card turns things into something else! That's what regular death is ALL about! People like to paint reverse death as meaning that you are somehow resisting the change death will bring, so the original effect that harms enemies would be very effective at conveying that.
14 - Temperance? - 4/10. Alright, if regular temperance is all about giving up your excess health, reverse temperance can be about indulging in way too many pills. I guess. It gets rid of my primary criticism that regular temperance allows you to choose whether to give up health.
15 - Devil? - 5/10. Remember, the devil is supposed to show you a feeling of powerless dependency. So reversed it could be like you're breaking the chains, so this is fine. I would have made this the inverse empress, personally. But it works well enough.
16 - Tower? - 5/10. The tower is destructive, this creates a bunch of garbage. Sure, this is a valid interpretation, and honestly a lot easier to stomach than the stuff a lot of websites propose. It's fine. It's not clever, but it's fine.
